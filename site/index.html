Perp delta hedging for binary-option inventory

When users buy YES on a 5-minute BTC binary, the house is short YES — and owns a directional bet on Bitcoin it never chose. This sidecar reads that inventory read-only, computes an aggregate delta, and holds an offsetting perp position. Below is its real decision path replayed over real Binance 1-second data.

Why it stands down more than it trades

A hedge that fires on every wobble pays fees for nothing. Two gates must both open before a single order is placed — realised volatility has to breach a threshold and exposure has to clear an inventory floor. In this replay the hedger is armed for roughly a third of ticks; the rest of the time idleReason names which gate is holding it back.

The book here is generated — no live exchange is reachable from a static page. Everything acting on it is the shipped code: GamebullInventorySource → Gate → Hedger → DryRunVenue, over real Binance 1-second klines. Regenerate with npx tsx scripts/demo-timeline.ts.

What a perp can and cannot do

A perpetual future is linear — Γ ≡ 0 by definition. A digital's value near expiry is a step, its delta diverging as φ(d)/(Sσ√τ). No linear instrument replicates a discontinuity, and an A/B confirmed it: the worst-case window went −$94.5 → −$94.5, a difference of exactly 0.0.

So the service does the job a perp can do — hedge delta — and does it well: 33–37% of P&L dispersion removed, worst case −$115 → +$85/+$109. Terminal gamma is handled in quoting instead.
